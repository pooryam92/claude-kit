# Brainstorm

The user describes a goal, a problem, or a thing. Five steps, in order, reasoning step by step through each in your thinking. No research. The shape matters because a model asked for ideas in one go gives local variations on its first thought; the steps below are the ones that measurably widen the set.

## Step 1. First principles

Strip the request down to its core.

- Write down what it assumes: the form, how it is done today, who does it, when, with what.
- For each assumption ask whether it is true by necessity or only habit. Drop the habits.
- Ask why until the answer stops changing. What is the person really after, and for whom?
- State the core in one sentence: the irreducible need, with no solution in it.

## Step 2. Personas

Invent three ordinary people who meet this core in their own life or trade, far from the user's field. Not founders, not famous names, not experts in the topic: ordinary personas produce more varied ideas than celebrated ones because they bring their own constraints instead of the field's conventions. A retired bus driver who organises the neighbourhood's street party; a nurse on night shifts who runs a sewing shop on the side; a farmer who sells at a Saturday market.

For each, one line: who they are, and what they would value or refuse when faced with the core.

## Step 3. Draft

Through each persona's eyes, write three or four short idea titles, ten in total. A title is five to ten words, no explanation yet. Stay in the persona: what would this person actually do about the core, with what they have?

## Step 4. Make bolder

Go through the ten titles and revise each to be bolder and more different from the others. For each title ask:

- Could this title have come without its persona? If yes, it is a generic answer wearing a costume; push it until it could not.
- Is it a variation of another title on the list? If yes, move it further away or replace it.
- What is the version of this that the user would call too far? Write that one down next to it; the bold version often sits between the two.

No two titles may share a mechanism. Keep the ten.

## Step 5. Expand five

Pick the five titles that differ most from each other and from what the user already has. Expand them one at a time, each finished before the next: a name, two or three sentences on what it is, and one line on why it serves the core.

## Answer

- The core, one sentence.
- The three personas, one line each.
- The ten bold titles, numbered, so the user can point at one not expanded.
- The five expanded ideas.

Follow-ups:

- "More": three new personas, ten new titles, five new expansions on the same core.
- "Go deeper on 3" or "expand title 7": expand that one.
- A corrected core: redo from step 2 with the user's sentence.
